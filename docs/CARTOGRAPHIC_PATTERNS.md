# Cartographic visual details

Added 9 November 2026 from the user's cartography references.

- **Hero:** a muted gold contour field behind the existing animated terrain mesh. The left reading area remains quiet.
- **Explorer, timeline, community, and notebook headings:** decorative contour banners with theme-specific contrast and a mobile fade.
- **Field notebook:** a cropped background from the supplied 1946 Arlington/Belmont/Lexington topographic map. It is treated as decorative imagery, not a map of the user's observations.
- **Home discovery links:** contour details on the notebook link and small geological hatch marks on the explorer link.
- **“Read the layers” illustration:** dots, discontinuous horizontal marks, brickwork, and crystalline hatching distinguish the four decorative strata.

The course reference is David Amitrano, *Cartographie Géologique, Support de Cours et Travaux Dirigés*, 2020–2021, supplied as `poly_carto_2020_hal.pdf`, HAL hal-03273240. Relevant visual concepts were reviewed on PDF pages 9 (relief and contour projection), 12 (orientation of geological planes), and 16 (map and geological section). The supplied contour examples, layer-pattern block, and historical map were also reviewed.

Nine original SVG patterns are generated by `tools/generate-cartography.mjs`. The contour field follows equal-height isolines of an original synthetic terrain, with heavier index contours and survey crosses. Geological hatches are redrawn vector motifs inspired by the references. They are decorative, not scientific legends assigning named lithologies to the site's illustrated layers. The existing scientific timescale colors, illustrations' motion, and text remain unchanged.

The supplied historical JPG is copied unchanged to `assets/patterns/field-map-1946.jpg`; display contrast and color treatment are applied in CSS. Other reference scans and PDF pages are not served as website files. Source graphics stay in their original locations.

`patterns.css` contains placement, contrast, fading, mobile, RTL, and forced-colors adjustments. Patterns are noninteractive pseudo-elements, do not enter the accessibility tree, and do not cover form or reading surfaces. Regenerate the SVG files with `node tools/generate-cartography.mjs`.

For direct opening of `index.html`, the same generator also writes `pattern-assets.css`, embedding the SVGs as data URLs. All decorative SVG masks reference those variables, avoiding browser restrictions on external `file://` mask images. The historical JPG remains a normal background image with a CSS gradient mask.

## Additional section motifs

- **Dictionary:** a cluster of outlined prismatic mineral crystals, fading toward the heading text.
- **Research:** curved sedimentary beds with a small offset and a dashed fault trace.
- **Community:** a dendritic drainage network suggesting field connections and natural branching.
- **Homepage notebook link:** a compass rose replaces the earlier contour watermark; the timeline link gains a small folded-bed detail.

These details are placed in heading banners or card corners. Article definitions, chat, inputs, and the already detailed hero are not given additional overlays. Every new mask uses the embedded source, with theme, mobile, RTL, and forced-colors handling.

## Relief-to-contour illustration

The supplied `images.jpg` was used as the reference for an enhanced, generated 1536 × 1024 transparent PNG, `assets/relief-contour-guide.png`. Its relief view and projected contour map are presented in the Field notebook's collapsible **Read the terrain** section, after the notebook controls. The original source image is preserved. The new artwork has no baked-in text; the heading, explanation, view labels, and alternative text are localized in English, French, and Arabic. It illustrates contour correspondence without assigning survey heights or a real geographic location.
